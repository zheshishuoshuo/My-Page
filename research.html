Research

Statistical Strong Lensing

I study how strong gravitational lenses, especially lensed quasars, can be used to infer galaxy mass distributions and separate stellar and dark matter components at the population level.

Questions

What I am trying to measure

My work connects lensing observables, microlensing statistics, and survey selection effects to physical models of lens-galaxy populations.

Lens-Galaxy Mass Profiles

How do strong-lens galaxies distribute mass across stellar and dark matter components?

Microlensing Statistics

What can quasar microlensing reveal about stellar mass fractions in lens galaxies?

Selection Effects

How do lens-finding methods shape the population-level conclusions we can safely draw?

Projects

Current and Recent Projects

2026 - Present

Statistical Analysis of Gaia Multi-Peak Lenses

I analyze lensed quasars selected with the Gaia Multi-Peak method, develop statistical methods to infer lens-galaxy mass distributions from lensing observables, and investigate how selection effects impact population-level inference.

2024 - 2026

Statistical Strong Lensing with Microlensed Quasars

This project develops hierarchical Bayesian methods for population-level inference with lensed quasars, with a focus on separating stellar mass components from dark matter density profiles in strong-lens galaxies.

2023 - 2024

FAST HI Data Analysis and High-Velocity Clouds

During my undergraduate research at Xiamen University, I processed large-area FAST HI survey data, produced maps of Galactic high-velocity cloud structures, and built Python workflows for spectral-line data processing and visualization.
